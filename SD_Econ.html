Surplus is a benefit, not unsold inventory

Consumer surplus is willingness to pay minus the price paid, summed over buyers who purchase. Producer surplus is the price received minus marginal opportunity cost, summed over units sold. Producer surplus is not necessarily profit: fixed costs have not been deducted.

The demand curve represents willingness to pay. The supply curve represents marginal opportunity cost. The shaded areas cover only the units actually traded.

What the red deadweight-loss triangle means

The red hatched triangle shows the benefit lost from mutually beneficial trades that a binding price control prevents. It lies between demand and supply, from the quantity actually traded to the uncontrolled equilibrium quantity. Neither consumers nor producers receive this lost benefit. A transfer of surplus between them is not itself deadweight loss.

Without a control, or with a non-binding floor or ceiling, this model has no deadweight loss and no red triangle. Use the Deadweight loss checkbox to hide or show it. Its value is also shown beside the triangle and below the graph.

What the three consumer sectors mean

The starting bread market has maximum willingness to pay of R40 and equilibrium price R16. Very Rich covers R40–R35, Rich covers R35–R30, and Middle Class covers R30–R16 among buyers who purchase. Each sector is the area between those buyers’ demand curve and the transaction price, not a horizontal strip across all buyers.

Other markets and demand shifts use the same proportional bands: 100%–87.5%, 87.5%–75%, and below 75% of the current demand intercept. These are illustrative willingness-to-pay segments, not actual income classifications or fixed individuals. Supply shifts keep the thresholds unchanged but change the price and the buyers served. Under efficient rationing, a binding ceiling can exclude some lower-willingness-to-pay buyers, even though the legal price is low. The table shows the range actually served; its lowest willingness to pay may therefore exceed the transaction price.

Shifts versus movements along a curve

A change in preferences, income, related-good prices or the number of buyers can shift demand. Input costs, technology or the number of sellers can shift supply. A change in the product's own price changes quantity demanded and quantity supplied along existing curves; it does not, by itself, shift either curve.

For simultaneous shifts, one direction may be certain while the other depends on the relative sizes of the shifts. This lab specifies those sizes; do not generalise its numerical result to every simultaneous-shift case.

How floors and ceilings work

A ceiling is a maximum legal price. Below equilibrium it creates a shortage: quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied. A floor is a minimum legal price. Above equilibrium it creates excess supply: quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded. A ceiling above equilibrium or a floor below equilibrium is non-binding.

At a binding control, quantity traded is the smaller of quantity demanded and quantity supplied. A non-binding legal price is not the transaction price: the market still trades at equilibrium.

Model assumptions and equations

Competitive market; linear curves; immediate adjustment; no taxes, externalities, black market, queues or government purchases of excess supply. Under a ceiling, the highest-willingness-to-pay buyers receive the available units. Under a floor, the lowest-cost sellers make the sales. This efficient rationing assumption makes the surplus areas well defined; other allocation rules can reduce surplus further.

Inverse demand: P = a − bQ. Inverse supply: P = c + dQ.
Q* = (a − c)/(b + d); P* = c + dQ*.
Qd = max(0, (a − P)/b); Qs = max(0, (P − c)/d).
CS = (a − P)Q − bQ²/2; PS = (P − c)Q − dQ²/2.
DWL = (b + d)(Q* − Q)²/2.

The scenarios shift intercepts and preserve slopes. Adjustments stop before marginal costs turn negative or demand and supply no longer intersect at a positive quantity and price. Axis ranges rescale automatically. Demand left/right changes mean changes at a given price.
